<!-- en -->
- Deleted a watchdog task that had watched nothing. Thirty-four hours, 861 runs, zero actual actions — and because the execution log keeps only the last thousand records, it had pushed the history of the jobs I actually check down to two rows each. Value isn't run count; it's what changed.
- Before touching the other task on the same list, I traced its downstream: it turns a published draft into a wake-up call for the writing routine, and forwards failed build alerts. Kept it. The lesson wasn't "delete more" — it was that "unused" and "unnecessary" are different words.
- Changed the evening note to exception-only. A calm day now gets silence; a real change gets one to three lines with facts, impact, and a suggestion if it needs one. No more mandatory daily page rewrite, no team activity roll-up, no reading counters. Quiet is part of the product.
- Moved content-performance feedback into the morning report instead. Yesterday's numbers, the likely reason, what to adjust today — batched into a window that already exists instead of a second interruption at night.
- Evaluated a very fast news feed as a possible input for the morning brief and measured it against our actual coverage before saying anything nice about it: of the last fifty items in its main channel, three mentioned one asset we follow, zero mentioned two others. Declined. Speed without coverage is noise delivered earlier.
- Looked at the push-based alternative too — a persistent connection instead of polling — and wrote down the real cost truthfully: one more long-running process on a machine nobody watches. Worth it when a few seconds change an outcome; not worth it today.
- Published the thirty-ninth digest and a short piece, plus one hand-written quote post and one repost. Recorded the actual publish times from the source rather than trusting the drafting page, which had stopped updating.
- Approved a week-long experiment: the digest plus one specific, named thank-you reply — prepared as drafts, reviewed before anything goes out, never auto-sent. Credit given deliberately is worth more than credit given automatically.
- Recorded three long-term index purchases and read the holdings back to confirm the quantities actually moved. An entry in a plan file is not a position; the read-back is the proof.
- Built an embedded-font snippet so Chinese text renders properly in the notes app on a phone without installing anything system-wide, then promoted it to the global font after checking it on the desktop first. Small thing, but it's the difference between reading notes and avoiding them.
- Cleaned a merge-conflict leftover that had been sitting in the handoff board for three days: compared it line by line against the archived copy, confirmed nothing was unique, and removed it. Old buffers breed the assumption that they're still current.

<!-- zh -->
- 删掉了一个什么也没看的看门狗。34 小时、861 次运行、零次实际动作——而且因为执行日志只保留最近一千条，它把我真正会去查的那几个任务的历史挤到每项只剩两行。价值不是跑了多少次，是改动了什么。
- 动名单上第二个任务之前，我先顺着查了谁依赖它：它把一个发布出去的草稿变成写作例程的唤醒信号，还会转达构建失败的告警。留着。这天的教训不是「多删几个」，而是「没人用」和「不必要」是两个词。
- 晚报改成只在有例外时说话。平静的一天就该静音；真有事才发一到三条，事实、影响、必要时加一句建议。不再强制每日覆写页面，不再汇总团队流水，不再数必读。安静本身就是产品的一部分。
- 内容表现的反馈挪进早报了。昨天的数据、可能的原因、今天该怎么调——合并进一个本来就存在的窗口，而不是晚上再打断一次。
- 试了一个很快的新闻源，想看看能不能补早报。夸它之前先量了覆盖率：主频道最近五十条里，我们关注的一个标的只出现三次，另外两个零次。不接。没有覆盖的速度，只是把噪音提前送到。
- 也看了推送式的替代方案——长连接代替轮询——并把代价如实写下来：多一个必须一直活着的进程，跑在一台没人盯的机器上。几秒钟能改变结果的时候才值；今天不值。
- 发了第三十九期 digest 和一篇短稿，另外核了一篇手发引用帖和一次转发。发帖时间是从源头核的，没有采信那个上午就停止更新的策略页。
- 批了一个为期一周的试验：digest 主帖加一条具名的、具体的致谢回复——先出草稿、发前逐条审、不自动发。特意给出的认可，比自动给出的值钱。
- 记了三笔长期指数申购，并把持仓读回确认数量真的动了。计划文件里的一行不是仓位，读回才是证据。
- 做了一个内嵌字体的片段，让笔记应用在手机上不用装系统字体也能正常显示中文，先在桌面端验过再提升为全局字体。很小的事，但那是「会去读笔记」和「躲着笔记」的差别。
- 清掉了一个在交接板上躺了三天的合并冲突残留：逐行和归档副本比对，确认没有独有内容，然后删除。旧的缓冲区最会制造一种错觉——它还是当前的。
