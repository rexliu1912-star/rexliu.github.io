The best trading rule I wrote this year has nothing to do with markets.

It's about reverse parking: "When the left mirror meets the yellow line, turn the wheel all the way right."

I wrote it in my journal on July 5. It got me through the parking test. Four months later, it's what finally steadied my trading.

Here's why.

In June I moved back to Jiashan, a small town near Shanghai, and signed up for driving school. I didn't want to. Long hours in a car made me uneasy. So I set one goal, get the licence, and ignored everything else.

Driving school doesn't care how you feel. You memorise reference points. You drive the exam route until it's boring. On July 29 I nearly clipped a car on my left, and that night I wrote one conclusion: know the route by heart.

My trading that summer ran on the exact opposite.

I bought two China tech ETFs too heavily on day one, so later buys barely moved my cost. A-shares swung too fast for me to hold, so I dumped everything at once. I watched too many tickers and missed the ones I knew. I followed other people's calls. My own weekly review put it bluntly: "I trusted other people's opinions too much."

And I sold on mood. "My selling has always been random," I wrote in mid-September.

On the exam, any one of those fails you on the spot. In the market they bleed you slowly. Slow pain is easy to ignore, so I kept doing them.

The real lesson came on August 6, at the parking test.

First run, I likely misheard an instruction and crossed the line on the S-curve. Fail. Second run, during side parking, my mind drifted back to that mistake. I missed my mark. I turned harder, stayed inside the line, and passed.

That night I didn't write about the S-curve. I wrote about the second run. Slow down. Drop the last miss. Do the next item.

In trading I did the reverse. In late September my review listed it: chasing names I'd already missed, still unwilling to let them go, forcing entries with no buy point, hoping Q4 would bail me out.

On the test, a mistake ends at the cone. In the market, I carried every miss into the next trade.

So in September I started running my accounts like driving school.

I split them by job: US stocks, crypto, A-shares. A trading account takes the signal, exits, and waits for the next one. I wrote a plan every morning before the open. I started a trading journal, mostly so it would slow me down.

On September 28 I sold my Hang Seng Tech position and quit A-share single stocks. "To keep it simple, don't touch single stocks. Just buy the index." Most of my swing trades now go to gold and Nasdaq ETFs. My index buying runs on a schedule, and I worry less about the premium.

In October I tested one last clever idea: fat-finger orders. You park small buy orders far below the market and wait for a sudden price spike, often caused by someone's typo or a flash crash, to fill them. I spent a week on it. On October 21 I quit. Without an edge, it was just anxious waiting.

The surprise was that my mood changed before my returns did.

June felt empty. I wasn't staring at candles every morning anymore, and the quiet felt wrong. July was anxious. By early September I admitted that copying other people's strategies lost money anyway. "Winning didn't feel good. Losing felt worse."

By September 24 my journal entry was short: "My mindset is much better. Holding positions feels comfortable."

Driving followed the same curve. On September 1 I drove at night and was too scared to change lanes on main roads. I kept driving anyway. By mid-October reversing and picking routes felt easy. I still drive when traffic is light. I want to trade that way too.

I won't pretend it's solved.

On October 17 I wrote that learning to drive might be my only real achievement this half year. My assets stood still. I missed a lot. The system is simpler, but the results haven't proved it yet.

A licence isn't skill either. I got mine on August 7. Weeks later I was still braking badly and fumbling the final park.

But I now ask three questions before every trade.

Is my mirror on the line, or am I going on feel? Do I know this road, or did I just watch someone else make money on it? Is the last miss still in the car?

One wrong answer, and I don't move.

Feel is what you trust when you haven't drawn the line yet.
